# Animal Crossing: New Horizons Manual APWorld



Hiiiiii! This is my Archipelago manual for Animal Crossing: New Horizons. This is the first manual I've ever developed, so there may be some issues, technical or gameplay-wise. You can contact me on Discord, @courtneyevie if you notice any.



## What can I choose as my goal?

* Credits: Complete Project K and roll the credits
* Complete Museum (No Art): Donate all fish, bugs, sea creatures, and fossils to the museum
* Complete Museum (With Art): Donate all fish, bugs, sea creatures, fossils, and works of art to the museum
* All Home Loans: Pay off all of your home loans, not including storage expansions



## What items can I receive?

* Access to crafting, buying, and using tools i.e. Fishing Rod or Net
* The ability to interact with different characters, i.e. Redd or Daisy Mae
* Different months to play in
* Certain purchaseable collections of DIY/cooking recipes, i.e. DIY for Beginners or Test Your DIY Skills
* The ability to invite new villagers to the island
* Certain upgrades, i.e. Tool Ring or Inventory Space
* The ability to purchase certain helpful items, i.e. Nook Miles Ticket, Customization Kit
* Fun! (which is just filler)
* Traps



## Uhhh, what was that last one? Traps?

Yep! One of your YAML options, as with most games, is trap percentage, or the number of filler items that are replaced with traps! If it is set to a number above 0, then these are the five traps you will see:

* New Day Trap: Save and quit and time travel to the next day that you have unlocked
* Broke Trap: Take 10,000 bells out of your pockets (or all of them if you don't have 10,000 bells on you) and put them in the nearest bin. This does not affect bells dropped on the floor, in the ABD, or invested in turnips
* Go Home Trap: Call the Rescue Service and have them take you back to your home
* Tool Trap: Take your most recently used tool and put it in the nearest bin
* Bad Friend Trap: Locate your nearest villager, smack them over the head with a net until they get angry, and don't apologise. If you don't have access to a net, just push them around a bit, it does the same thing.



## So what can I do without needing an item?

Really great question, actually. As a general rule of thumb, if something is in logic, you can assume that anything you need for it that you don't have as an item, isn't an item. Other things that don't require an item include but aren't limited to participating in holidays, interacting with characters that don't have an item like Harv or Mable, opening and using facilities such as the museum or Able Sisters, etc. I would recommend having a look at the logic spreadsheet I made and seeing what items actually exist. The biggest one that needs clarification is crafting the flimsy fishing rod. It's automatically crafted as part of the tutorial so I didn't restrict the crafting of it to an item, otherwise you just couldn't get any DIY recipe locations without getting the fishing rod. You still need the item to buy or use it but crafting it is okay.



## What can I do to send items to other games?

* Craft guaranteed DIY recipes for the first time. A guaranteed DIY recipe is one that doesn't rely on any luck, like message bottles or balloons, but instead are obtained by perfectly controllable means. Recipes given by Tom Nook, purchaseable from Nook's Cranny, obtainable through simple methods i.e. digging up a manila clam for the first time, or obtained from simple holiday quests all give items. The only exception is the golden tools, because while these are possible, they require a LOT of effort. The least effort is the golden shovel since you can time travel, but I decided that if you have one, you have to have them all. Besides, you'd need to find a golden nugget anyway.
* Complete the first stage of Nook Miles goals. Except for the five Nook Miles goals that are not listed as locations. These are Golden Milestone for the same reasons as why the DIY recipes are excluded, Island and Yourland because it requires online play, Host the Most for the same reason, That's One Smart Phone because I genuinely don't understand why it's not a higher stage for NookPhone Life, and Remarkable Remodeler because it costs 1,097,000 bells and that amount of effort should not be forced upon anyone who does not choose the All Home Loans goal. The rest are all locations, and a shocking amount are available right from the start of the game, but they aren't very tedious to complete. Heck, a fair few of them are nearly automatic.
* Donating fish to the museum (optional under YAML option Fishsanity)
* Donating bugs to the museum (optional under YAML option Bugsanity)
* Donating sea creatures to the museum (optional under YAML option Seasanity)
* Obtaining unique villager photos (optional under YAML option Photosanity, because I thought that would be real fun)

In addition to the options listed above, there is also an option to disable post-game checks, or locations that cannot be reached until completing Project K. These locations include cooking recipes, as well as certain Nook Miles goals that involve post-game only mechanics like Kapp'n, the hotel, the Roost, etc.

## Are there any important rules I need to follow?

1. Start from a fresh save file. Unfortunately this means if you only have one Switch, you can't play this without resetting your island. I might make another version of the apworld that doesn't require that in the future.
2. Your YAML settings. You can control your goal, the hemisphere you're playing in (which affects requirements for certain Nook Miles goals and fish/bug/sea creature donation locations), what percentage of your filler gets replaced with traps, and certain options on what locations to include as specified more above.
3. Speaking of the hemisphere, make sure, when starting your game, to select the same hemisphere as is specified in your YAML. Otherwise all month requirements will be offset by six months and that's just going to be confusing. Plus, no one wants to start playing in August.
4. And speaking of starting your game, you should set your Switch's date to the 1st of January before you begin playing. You start being able to play in January but other months are unlocked through items. You can play in any year though, past present or future, so if you run out of days you can just skip forward a year. Do be careful about that though. Weeds and all. personally i actually kinda like weeding in new horizons, it's pretty chill
5. You should set your birthday to... your birthday. The Nook Miles goal that relies on your birthday is internally set to require all month items, so you're free to set it to your real-world birthday if you'd like! Or January 31st. Either works. 99% chance it's a filler item anyway.
6. You're allowed to time travel. Actually, scratch that, you SHOULD time travel. Even in an async, don't BK someone for seven real-world months. I hope I didn't need to say that.
7. If you're playing with traps, I recommend buying a bin as soon as you can so that you can follow through on the Broke Trap and Tool Trap. You can redeem Nook Miles for one at the Nook Stop. If you get one of these traps before getting a bin, just drop the items on the floor until a bin shows up. The bin rule only applies to the traps, so a flimsy fishing rod you crafted before getting the fishing rod item or a shovel you received from a very kind Pashmina don't need to be thrown away, you can just drop them or put them in storage until you can use them.
8. Please don't connect to a save file that's already completed the game and drop a ton of really good late-game stuff at the start. That kinda defeats the point. This apworld was developed with the intention of playing the whole game from scratch. You're totally free to have a friend move onto your island and play in local co-op, but nothing in the apworld is terribly difficult on your own. MAYBE home loans, but if you're worried about that then you're absolutely not forced to choose it as your goal. Some locations do require quite a few bells, one requires 98,000 and three require upgrading Nook's Cranny which I think costs 300,000, but the internal requirements take this into account.
9. And finally... this is a lot. And, especially in a manual, I do not expect anyone to do it all perfectly, so if you mess up and send the wrong check, or use an item you didn't have yet, or accidentally time travel to February 1st when you don't have February, or accidentally submit all of the items for one of the first three villager homes before you have the Progressive Villager items required to do so, do not, and I mean do NOT, beat yourself up about it. Trust me, it'll happen. It happens to me every time I play and I made the thing. Just correct it if possible (i.e. storing or dropping a tool you didn't mean to craft until you get the item for it, save quit and time travel back or forward to a day you are able to play, etc.) and keep playing as usual.
